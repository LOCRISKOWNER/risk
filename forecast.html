How the mechanism is classified. Each estimate gets one tag.
  • Misalignment / loss of control the question or speaker specifies AI takeover, misaligned or power-seeking AI, or humans losing control of AI.
  • Mixed the source says explicitly that the number covers both misaligned AI and human misuse.
  • ⚠ Undetermined labelled AI catastrophe, extinction or doom without saying whether misalignment or misuse is meant. Where context points one way, a note says so, but the tag stays.

Several figures come from secondary reports rather than the primary document; these are marked “via secondary report” in the table.

I’ve collected published probabilities that AI causes human extinction, permanent disempowerment or a global catastrophe, focusing on risk from misaligned AI and loss of control. Where a number is labelled only as “AI extinction” or “AI catastrophe” I include it and flag that the mechanism is undetermined.

What the collection shows

  1. Most headline numbers don’t specify misalignment. Of the figures most often quoted in policy settings (Hinton 10–20%, Amodei 10–25%, the XPT tournament, the FRI LEAP panel, the AI Impacts headline question), none separates loss of control from misuse. Fewer than a third of the estimates here are cleanly about misalignment.
  2. Where misalignment is specified, estimates cluster at 10–35%. Ord 10%, Carlsmith >10%, Christiano 22% for takeover, Samotsvety 25%, Lifland 35%, Shlegeris 35% over ten years. AI researchers asked about “inability to control” give a median of 9–10%. The outliers are Yudkowsky (~99.5%) and the Conjecture staff survey (70%).
  3. Group disagreement spans two orders of magnitude. Superforecasters (0.38% AI extinction by 2100; 2.4% AI catastrophe by 2100 in 2026) and FRI’s sceptic group (0.1%; nine of its eleven members were superforecasters) sit far below AI safety researchers (25–35%). FRI’s own conditional questions suggest much of the gap is about how fast capabilities arrive: experts’ AI-catastrophe forecast for 2100 rises from 2% under slow progress to 10% under rapid progress.
  4. Question wording moves the answer about as much as time does. In the 2022 and 2023 AI Impacts surveys the narrower “inability to control” question got a higher median (10%) than the broader “AI advances causing extinction” question (5%), which is logically odd given the first is a subset. In the 2024 survey the order flipped (9% vs 10%).
  5. The researcher median has drifted up; forecaster numbers have not. The AI Impacts headline question doubled from 5% (2023) to 10% (fielded December 2024). FRI’s 2026 expert panel gives 5% for an AI catastrophe by 2100, below the 12% from XPT experts in 2022, though the panels differ.

FRI LEAP wave 12 in detail

Wave 12 (August–September 2026) asks one question: the probability of an AI-caused catastrophe by 31 December 2050, defined as an event in which more than 10% of the people alive at the start of a five-year period die. It reports no extinction or loss-of-control figure and no misalignment-versus-misuse split, so every number here is ⚠ Undetermined. The first three baseline figures are the ones plotted above; the general public, which is not a forecaster group, and the policy scenarios appear only here. All figures are medians.

Notes on interpretation

Horizons. Ord’s figure is for this century, Carlsmith’s for 2070, the tournaments’ for 2100, Hinton’s for 30 years, Hubinger’s and Shlegeris’s for ten years, and many personal numbers have no horizon at all. A 10% figure over ten years implies far higher annual hazard than 10% by 2100.

Outcome severity. “Extinction”, “existential catastrophe” (which includes permanent disempowerment and lock-in), and “catastrophe” (FRI: more than 10% of people die within five years) are different events. FRI’s XPT gives roughly a 5:1 ratio of AI catastrophe to AI extinction for superforecasters, and 4:1 for experts.

Selection. Safety-researcher surveys (Bensinger 2021, Conjecture 2023, Summit on Existential Security 2026) select for concern; AI Impacts had a 10–17% response rate, though the authors report that respondents attentive and inattentive to the topic gave similar medians.

Mixed figures. Bengio’s 20% explicitly includes humans turning AI against humanity. Christiano’s 46% “irreversibly messed up” includes takeover (22%) plus non-takeover paths. These are tagged mixed, not undetermined.
